Understanding Collagen: Sources & Types

🐟 Marine Collagen

Extracted primarily from the skin and scales of fish, marine collagen is celebrated for its exceptional bioavailability. Consisting almost entirely of Type I collagen, it is the premier choice for restoring dermal elasticity, reducing fine lines, and strengthening brittle nails.

🐄 Bovine Collagen

Sourced from pasture-raised cattle, bovine collagen delivers a robust blend of Types I and III. While it supports radiant skin, its higher glycine and proline profile makes it superior for joint integrity, tendon resilience, and soothing gut lining inflammation.

🐔 Chicken Cartilage

Sourced predominantly from poultry cartilage, chicken collagen is naturally concentrated with Type II collagen. It is targeted specifically at protecting synovial fluid, reducing morning joint crepitus, and calming activity-induced stiffness.

Type I The Beauty Builder

Makes up 90% of your body's natural collagen. Vital for dermal suppleness, hair thickness, and bone structure.

Sources: Marine, Bovine.

Type II The Joint Protector

Found primarily in articular cartilage. Critical for shock absorption, knee comfort, and reducing inflammation.

Sources: Chicken cartilage.

Type III The Tissue Healer

Forms the structural matrix of muscles, organs, and blood vessels alongside Type I.

Sources: Grass-fed bovine.

Types V & X The Enhancers

Type V supports hair follicle membranes; Type X stimulates new bone and cartilage formation in multi-blends.

Sources: Eggshell & Multi.

Which Collagen Source is Best for Perimenopause and Mature Skin?

During perimenopause and menopause, declining estrogen levels accelerate collagen loss, with up to 30% of dermal collagen disappearing in the first five years following menopause onset. For mature skin rejuvenation, clinical evidence heavily favours **Type I hydrolysed marine collagen peptides**. Marine collagen provides a high concentration of prolyl-hydroxyproline dipeptides that trigger fibroblasts in the dermis to synthesise new extracellular matrix. Pairing a 5g to 10g marine peptide dose with hyaluronic acid and vitamin C significantly counters age-related dermal thinning and moisture depletion.

How to Avoid White-Label and Filler-Heavy Collagen Brands

The UK supplement market contains numerous white-label brands that repackage low-purity, unverified animal byproducts with substantial marketing markups. When evaluating brands, watch for two key warning signs: proprietary blends that do not specify the exact milligram weight of collagen per serving, and excessive sweetening agents (such as maltodextrin or artificial syrups) designed to mask inferior sourcing. Look for certifications confirming pasture-raised cattle or MSC-certified wild marine catches, alongside third-party testing verifying that heavy metals fall well below UK Food Standards Agency limits.

Powder vs. Liquid vs. Gummies: The Active Yield Comparison

Supplement shoppers often fail to calculate the true active protein yield. While a box of liquid collagen sachets may seem comparable to a tub of hydrolysed powder, a 500g tub of pure bovine powder delivers 500 grams of pure peptide yield, whereas a standard box of liquid sachets or gummies often delivers less than 60 grams of total active protein per container. Chewable gummies represent the lowest active yield, often requiring you to consume up to 24g of refined sugar just to reach a baseline clinical dose.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a collagen supplement is good quality? +
Look for hydrolysed peptides (often termed collagen hydrolysate), third-party lab testing, and clear sourcing statements such as "grass-fed pasture-raised" for bovine, or "wild-caught MSC-certified" for marine. Avoid products that obscure their active collagen dose in proprietary filler blends.
Do collagen gummies really work as well as powders? +
Gummies are convenient and delicious, but physical size constraints limit most gummy servings to 500mg to 1,000mg (1g) of collagen, compared to 10,000mg (10g) in a standard scoop of powder. If you take gummies, consider them a light daily top-up rather than a full therapeutic joint or skin rebuilding dose.
Should I take collagen with Vitamin C? +
Yes. Vitamin C is a critical biological cofactor for prolyl hydroxylase and lysyl hydroxylase—the essential enzymes that synthesize and cross-link collagen in human tissues.
What is the best time of day to take collagen? +
Consistency matters far more than timing. Hydrolysed peptides are absorbed effectively whether taken with your morning coffee, mixed into an afternoon smoothie, or taken before bed to assist nighttime tissue recovery.
