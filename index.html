Understanding Collagen: Sources & Types

🐟 Marine Collagen

Extracted primarily from the skin and scales of fish, marine collagen is celebrated for its exceptional bioavailability (meaning your body absorbs it quickly and efficiently). It consists almost entirely of Type I collagen, making it the ultimate choice for enhancing skin elasticity, reducing fine lines, and strengthening hair and nails.

🐄 Bovine Collagen

Sourced from the hides of cows (look for grass-fed and pasture-raised), bovine collagen is a powerhouse blend of Types I and III. While it still offers fantastic skin and beauty benefits, it truly excels in full-body wellness—supporting joint health, aiding muscle recovery, and helping to repair the gut lining.

Type I The Beauty Builder

Makes up 90% of your body's natural collagen. It is essential for glowing skin, strong nails, thick hair, and robust bones and ligaments.

Type II The Joint Protector

Found predominantly in cartilage. It is crucial for joint flexibility, comfort, reducing inflammation, and supporting a healthy gut.

Type III The Muscle Healer

Supports the structure of muscles, organs, and blood vessels. It works closely with Type I to support cardiovascular health and skin elasticity.

Types V & X The Enhancers

Often found in multi-collagen blends. Type V supports cell surfaces and hair growth, while Type X is vital for bone and new cartilage formation.

Marine Collagen vs. Bovine Collagen

One of the most frequent questions buyers have is choosing between marine and bovine sources. Marine collagen (sourced from fish skin and scales) is highly bioavailable and primarily consists of Type I collagen, making it the preferred choice for skin rejuvenation, hair vitality, and nail strength. Bovine collagen (sourced from pasture-raised or grass-fed cattle) provides a robust blend of Type I and Type III collagen, which is highly beneficial for muscle recovery, joint support, and gut health.
