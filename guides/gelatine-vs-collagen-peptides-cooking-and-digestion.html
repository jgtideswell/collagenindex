Gelatine vs Hydrolysed Collagen: Gelling Properties, Digestion & Uses

Same Parent Protein, Totally Different Molecular Chain Lengths

Gelatine and hydrolysed collagen peptides come from the exact same bovine hides or fish skins and share the exact same amino acid profile. The sole difference is one extra enzymatic processing step—which completely changes how they behave in your kitchen and digestive tract.

Key Takeaways

  • Gelatine is partially hydrolysed (~50,000–100,000 Da), so it blooms in cold liquid and sets into firm jelly when cooled.
  • Collagen peptides are fully enzymatically hydrolysed (~2,000–3,000 Da), dissolving instantly in hot or cold water without ever gelling.
  • Gelatine digests more slowly, coating the stomach lining and providing longer satiety.
  • Use hydrolysed peptides for coffee and smoothies; use grass-fed gelatine for homemade gut-healing berry gummies and panna cotta.

1. From Native Collagen to Gelatine to Peptides

Raw animal collagen (~300,000 Da) is heated gently in water to unwind its triple helix into gelatine (~50,000–100,000 Da). Food-grade protease enzymes then snip gelatine into tiny hydrolysed peptides (~2,000 Da) that can no longer form a gel network.

2. Choosing the Right One for Your Goal

For fast plasma Pro-Hyp dipeptide spikes targeting skin elasticity and pre-workout tendons, choose hydrolysed peptides. For soothing gastric lining comfort, culinary jellies, or thick stews at ultra-low cost, grass-fed gelatine excels.

3. Homemade Vitamin-C Collagen Gummies Recipe Tip

Bloom 3 tablespoons of grass-fed gelatine in 250ml warm berry juice (rich in vitamin C), add 2 scoops of hydrolysed collagen peptides for extra protein punch, pour into silicone moulds, and chill for 2 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I put regular baking gelatine in my morning coffee?

Only if you drink it piping hot right away—as soon as the mug cools below 35°C, gelatine begins turning your coffee into lumpy jelly.

Does gelatine have the same skin benefits as collagen peptides?

Gelatine supplies the same glycine and proline amino acids, though fully hydrolysed peptides generate higher, faster intact Pro-Hyp dipeptide spikes in clinical trials.
