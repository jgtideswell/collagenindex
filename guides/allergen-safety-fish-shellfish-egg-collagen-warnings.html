Collagen Allergens Guide: Fish, Shellfish, Egg & Cross-Contamination Risks

Mapping Every UK Allergen Risk Across Marine, Multi, Bovine & Chicken Formulas

Collagen is derived from diverse animal tissues, meaning hidden allergens frequently catch sensitive buyers off guard—especially in five-source multi-collagen tubs or shared marine packaging lines.

Key Takeaways

  • Fish allergy: STRICTLY avoid all marine collagen and multi-collagen blends; choose pure single-source bovine or vegan builders.
  • Shellfish allergy: True marine collagen comes from fin-fish (not crustacean shellfish), yet shared marine processing facilities can carry cross-contact warnings—always check "May Contain" lines.
  • Egg allergy: Avoid multi-collagen blends containing Types V & X from eggshell membrane.
  • Alpha-gal syndrome (tick-bite mammalian meat allergy): Avoid bovine and porcine collagen; choose pure wild marine or chicken Type II.

2. Reading UK Bolded Allergen Declarations

Under UK Natasha’s Law and FSA allergen regulations, top 14 allergens—including FISH, CRUSTACEANS, EGGS, MILK, and SOYA—must be highlighted in BOLD in the ingredients list. Check both the main list and advisory "packed in a facility that handles..." statements.

3. Why Single-Ingredient Powders Are Safest

Pure one-ingredient hydrolysed bovine or marine powders eliminate surprise botanical, soy-lecithin, or milk-powder reactions completely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can people with shellfish allergy take wild cod marine collagen?

Biologically, fin-fish proteins differ from crustacean tropomyosin, so many shellfish-allergic people tolerate cod collagen—provided the brand confirms zero crustacean cross-contamination. Always verify with your allergist first.

Is pure collagen naturally gluten-free and dairy-free?

Yes! Pure animal collagen peptides contain zero gluten, zero casein, and zero lactose.
